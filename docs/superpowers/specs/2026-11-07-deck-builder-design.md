# Deck Builder — design

Date: 2026-11-07 · Branch: `deck-builder` (from `mods-expansion` 877e614)

## 1. Intent

A new Pod Programs page, **Deck Builder**, that generates strong Arena decks and
writes them to the player's save. The decks should be the ones an experienced
Arena player would pick for a matchup, or the best general picks. Every piece of
the loadout is filled in.

The same decks and loadouts also power the 5,000 Arena computer players. The
ladder then gets harder by deck quality, not only by power, and the top of the
ladder fields the strongest meta decks.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
- Generate many Arena decks from criteria, as a ranked list the player can keep
  scrolling. It is not limited to a top 3.
- Two build modes:
  - **What I own**, optionally allowing up to N missing pieces.
  - **Theoretical best**, where anything in the game may be used.
- Build against a target: general best, or a specific opponent from the
  player's matching list.
- Every pick comes with a short explanation, and every place where the data
  overrules an expert gets a stated reason.
- Write a chosen deck to the slots the player selects, adding missing pieces
  fully raised. Nothing else in the save changes.
- Give the Arena computer players full loadouts and a strength curve.

**Non-goals**
- Other modes (Subjugation, quests, towers). Subjugation is a possible later
  idea.
- Exact battle reproduction. The simulator only needs to rank decks correctly,
  not replay fights frame by frame.
- Editing the player's existing gear beyond what a written deck needs.

**Anti-goals** (failure even if it "works")
- Overwriting or changing a deck slot the player didn't select, or removing
  anything the player owns.
- Decks an experienced player would laugh at: damage-only stacks with no
  answer to Dauntless or revive, or wrong elements for the slot.
- The data quietly overruling an expert lineup without a reason the player can
  read.
- Explanations that are wrong about game mechanics. One draft was wrong this
  way: it claimed Compromise removes an active Dauntless. See §6.
- Shipping a page whose numbers are placeholders presented as real.

  - *Theoretical best*.
- **Against:**
  - *General best*.
  - *An opponent from my matching list.* This shows the current matching list
    with each opponent's points and lineup, read from the save or server the
    same way the game shows it.

**Criteria (the generator)**
- **Strategy:** any, stall, burst, Dauntless/invulnerability, Compromise
  counter, tank and provoke, mono-element.
- **Element by slot:** left, middle and right, each any or a specific element.
- **Must have these effects:** Compromise, Cleanse, Revive, Provoke, Dauntless,
  Indomitable, Usable from start.
- **Include / exclude:** costumes, characters and elements.
- **Minimum stats:** agility, HP, ATK and crit rate (sliders).
- **Sort by:**
  - best against this opponent (or general win rate when the target is
    general);
  - Force;
  - fewest pieces to add.

**Results**
- An endless, ranked list showing a total count, for example
  "18,640 decks · showing 1–6".
- The opponent, mode and sort show once, in the results bar, not on each card.
- Each card (v5) shows:
  - the rank number, the strategy name and **Wins %**: the simulated win
    chance against the opponent, or against the meta pool for General best;
  - three portraits, with the name under each portrait, the slot letter on the
    bottom edge and a NEW corner mark;
  - up to 3 effect tags on desktop, dropped on phones;
  - a reason capped at two lines;
  - a footer with Force, "to add" and "+ N variants".
- Near-duplicates fold into variants: the same three costumes with different
  gear or slot order.

**Detail panel** (opens from a card)
- The full loadout per character: costume, main weapon, 2 sub weapons,
  companion, 3 memoirs (set and main/sub stats), debris, karma, awakening and
  character board.
- "Why this deck": the plan in a few sentences.
- A reason for each character and each piece.
- **Data override boxes** wherever the data replaced an expert choice, with the
  reason. Example: set 7 also lowers the wearer's DEF by 30%, so set 17 was
  picked instead.
- A **Write** panel:
  - The deck slots show "Your deck: …" or "Empty".
  - The player ticks the target slots.
  - A summary lists what will be added and raised.
  - Then the WRITE button.

## 5. Build modes and "fully raised"

**What I own**
- Uses the save's inventory at its current levels.
- With N > 0, up to N missing pieces may be added. Added pieces are fully
  raised.
- What counts as one "piece": a costume, a weapon, a companion, a debris, or a
  memoir.

**Theoretical best**
- Anything in the game's master data is allowed, every piece is fully raised,
  and the player's inventory is ignored.

**Fully raised** means all of the following:
- **Level and rebirth:** max level, all limit breaks, rebirth/costume level 100.
- **Skills:** all skills at max level.
- **Awakening:** maxed.
- **Karma:** the best karma effects for the deck's plan, at their top values.
- **Character board and rank bonus:** fully done.
- **Weapons:** max level and limit breaks, max skill and ability levels, in
  their final evolution (EX weapons in their IV form).
- **Companion:** max level.
- **Debris:** owned.
- **Memoirs:** at +15 with chosen main and sub stats (below).

**Memoirs (the player's decision): follow the original game's rules, including
crit-rate, crit-damage and agility main stats.**
- Bunker's server never rolls those mains, but master data has them
  (`EntityMPartsStatusMainTable`: kind 1 agility, 3 crit damage, 4 crit rate),
  and the save stores a memoir's main-stat id. Writing them is therefore
  possible.
- Main and sub values use the original game's maxima at +15:
  - crit rate 20%, crit damage 25%, agility 120;
  - ATK/DEF flat 610; ATK%, DEF% and HP% 19.5%; HP flat 6,600;
  - up to 4 subs, duplicates allowed, values at the level-12 step.
  The builder confirms these against master data's numerical functions, which
  win over the wiki values.
- The optimiser picks mains and subs per character role. Attackers come out
  around 50%+ crit rate and 200%+ crit damage.
- **"What I own" memoirs:**
  - The player's own memoirs keep their rolled stats.
  - Memoirs added as missing pieces follow the same theoretical-best rules.
  - My call: an added memoir is a new memoir. Existing memoirs are never
    re-rolled.

**EX characters are included:**
- Reborn 41000–45003 (each character's Recollections of Dusk end-of-story
  costume) and Frozen-Heart 51001–55003.
- EX weapons in their IV form, with their extra effects. For example,
  Glimmering Stephanos IV revives at 50% HP and Blade of Parting IV gives
  agility +20%.
- Collab characters (2B, 9S, A2, Zero, Emil) are included like any other
  playable costume if master data marks them playable. My call, since the
  question wasn't answered.

## 6. How a deck is chosen

**Who decides what (the player's rule)**
- **Experts pick the lineup** (which characters, in which slots) and the data
  picks everything else: weapons, memoirs, companion, debris and karma.
- The data may **overrule an expert lineup choice only with a data-backed
  reason**, shown in the UI.
- Expert knowledge comes from the research set: the nierrein PvP tier lists
  and Arena loadouts, plus 17 distinct decks read from Arena videos (§10).

**The engine**

A status-aware Arena simulator ranks candidate decks. A static score alone
ranked meta picks badly; for example, it put New Year's Belle Sarafa 216th. The
simulator models the following.

*Damage, elements and crits*
- Element advantage with Arena's coefficient group 3: ×1.2 advantage and ×0.8
  disadvantage, where light and dark hit each other for ×1.2.
- Crits.
- All stat layers: costume, rebirth, rank bonus, character board, level bonus,
  awakening, karma, weapons, memoirs (set bonuses and main/sub stats),
  companion and debris.

*Statuses*
- **Compromised (43):** blocks *new* beneficial statuses only. It does not
  remove active ones, and it doesn't block ailments or Crush.
- **Dauntless (44):** HP can't drop below 1.
- **Defiance (45):** evasion.
- **Provoke (24):** draws attacks.
- **Indomitable (18):** cuts damage from the next N hits.
- **Crush (30–34):** cleansable per the data.
- **Revive.**
- **Cleanses:** remove 1–2 ailments.
- **Invulnerable (10):** nothing a player can obtain grants it.
- **Removing beneficial statuses:** nothing in the player pool does this.

*Timing*
- Weapon skill cooldowns tick at 60 fps; buff durations at 30 fps.
- The character skill gauge fills by +250 per weapon skill, +50–200 per normal
  attack and +400 per companion skill.
- "Usable from start" skills (a hidden passive on 24 costumes), plus Blighted
  Flowers' 30% head start and haste karma ×0.70.

*Combo counter*
- ×1.00 → ×2.00 at 19+; on-screen COMBO n uses table row n−1.
- It applies only to skills flagged as combo targets.

*Opening order, calibrated on video*
1. Start-of-battle skills that are usable from start fire right → left →
   middle. The attacker goes first when tapping by hand; the defender goes
   first on Auto.
2. After that, units take turns ordered by agility.
3. There is a 3 s wave-intro delay before the first skill.

**Fit so far**
- **Opening, against 21 video fight openings:**
  - attacker's first skill right in 90%;
  - order within the attacker's side right in 8/10;
  - first-skill timing off by 1.2 s on average.
- **Top 20 costumes:** 19 of 20 agree with the expert lists and the video
  decks. Yuletide Witch Saryu at 15 is the one unlisted pick.

**Scoring a candidate**

The score is the simulated win rate:
- against the chosen opponent, attacking or defending as chosen; or
- for **general best**, against a weighted pool of meta decks: the expert decks
  plus the video decks.

Ties break on Force.

**Ranking, expert vs data lineups (the player's decision):**
- The list ranks purely by simulated win rate.
- A lineup no expert picked that ranks above expert lineups gets a "data pick"
  box. It names the expert deck it beats and by how much, which is the
  data-backed reason the expert rule requires.
- Expert lineups that rank low stay in the list at their simulated rank.
- The §11 expert-agreement check counts costumes in the top 20, not whole
  lineups.

**The generator**
- Lineups are enumerated with expert-favoured costumes first and pruned by the
  criteria.
- Each lineup is equipped by the data.
- The top candidates are simulated, and more are simulated as the player
  scrolls.
- Near-duplicates fold into variants.
- How to make this fast enough is left to the builder (§12).

**Explanations**
- Each deck gets a strategy label and a one-line plan.
- Each pick gets a reason, taken from the simulator's view of what the piece
  does: its status, timing and element role.
- Explanations must agree with the mechanics above. For example, a Compromise
  counter works by landing Compromise *before* the enemy's Dauntless or revive
  goes up, or by outlasting it. It never removes an active one.

**Data contradictions with experts already found** (use them as override
reasons where they apply)
- Sarafa's refresh is dark-only.
- Compromise doesn't block ailments or Crush.
- Crush is cleansable.
- New Year's Boor Yudil's Fleetfoot lasts 60 s in data, not the 30 s in the
  text.
- The "hold the skill for the blink" rule isn't reproduced.

## 7. Writing to the save

1. The player selects one or more deck slots, by default the Arena attack and
   defense slots.
2. Only those slots change. The player's other decks are kept exactly as they
   are.
3. Before every write, take one Save Data snapshot, as Gift does.
4. Pieces the deck needs that the player lacks are added fully raised, as in §5.
5. Owned pieces are used as they are in "What I own".
6. In "Theoretical best", an owned piece below max is handled by kind:
   - **Weapons, memoirs, companions and debris** are left alone, and a
     separate, fully raised copy is added. My call: existing copies are never
     changed.
   - **Owned costumes and their character** (level, rebirth, skills,
     awakening, karma, character board, rank bonus) can't be copied. **The
     player decides each time** (their decision). The Write panel lists every
     owned costume or character it would raise, with current → max, and the
     player ticks which ones it may change.
   - When an item is unticked, it stays as it is, and the deck's score is
     shown again at the real levels before writing.
   - Raising a costume also strengthens the player's other decks that use it.
     The panel says so.
   - The "other slots byte-identical" check then applies to the deck slots
     themselves, not to costume records the player ticked.
7. If one owned item would be needed in two decks being written at once, add a
   copy where the game allows duplicates. Otherwise, say so before writing.
8. After writing, the game's deck power for the slot is updated the next time
   the client reports it. The page says this.
9. The write is refused, with the reason shown, when the inventory is full,
   when a piece can't be granted, or when the game is live in a way that blocks
   edits.

## 8. Arena computer players

- **Loadouts.** The 5,000 CPU players use Deck Builder decks with full
  loadouts:
  - companion, sub weapons, memoirs and debris;
  - karma, awakening and character board where the PvP deck proto carries them
    (`PvpDeckCharacter` in `server/proto/pvp.proto:81`).
  - Today the CPU struct (`ladder.go`) only has 3 costumes and 3 weapons, and
    `deck.go` sends an empty companion and thought. Both get extended.
- **Strength curve (the player's rule):**
  - Opponents scale to *the player's deck strength* (simulated quality), not
    only power.
  - A curve ramps quality up the ladder.
  - The top of the ladder always fields the meta "best of best" decks,
    whatever the player's deck is.
  - Lower ranks use weaker decks and less-raised gear near the player's level.
  - The existing `Strength` clamp (0.8–1.25) and point system keep working.
- **Where the decks come from.**
  - The Go server can't run the Python simulator in a match request.
  - My call: Deck Builder writes a ranked deck table, with loadouts and a
    quality score, into a data file the Go server reads.
  - A shipped default is generated at build time; Pod Programs regenerates it
    when master data changes.
  - The ladder samples from that table by rank and curve.
  - The exact file format and where it lives are left to the builder.
- **How the server knows the player's deck strength** (my call):
  - Pod Programs scores the player's current Arena attack and defense decks
    from the save, using the same simulator.
  - It writes that score per player into the data file, and re-scores whenever
    Deck Builder writes or the page is opened.
  - When no score exists, for example for a deck built in-game since the last
    scoring, the server maps the client-reported deck power to a quality score
    through the deck table.
  - The ladder curve stays per season. Only the bottom and middle of the curve
    move with the player's score; the top is fixed to meta decks.

## 9. Known gaps (calibration settings, not blockers)

- **Damage is about 3× too slow.**
  - The rank-1 Griff / New Year's Boor Yudil / Akeha deck wins at 49 s in the
    simulation vs 13 s on video, so its simulated attack win rate is only 0.25.
  - Tune before shipping.
- **Force.** Solved: ported exactly from the client's code (§3).
- **Combo rules are assumed.** Increment and reset are guesses.
- **Defender's first skill.** It varies from −1 to +5 s; which defender fires
  first isn't modelled precisely.
- **Some expert picks rank low:**
  - the agility snipers (Mechanical Hunter Gayle around 28th, Celebratory
    Hunter Gayle around 33rd);
  - Frozen-Heart Convict 063y, expert SSS but around 45th.
  - These stay in via the "experts pick the lineup" rule.

Every gap maps to a parameter in the simulator's settings. Tune them, then
re-check against §10.

## 11. Acceptance checks

- **Expert agreement.** With general best and theoretical best, the top 20
  costumes agree with the expert lists and video decks at least 19 of 20, as
  the prototype does.
- **Opening check.** It stays at least as good as the prototype's: 90% first
  skill, 1.2 s timing.
- **Win times.** After damage tuning, the video decks' win times are in the
  right range.
- **Write safety.**
  - Writing to a slot changes only that slot and adds only the listed pieces.
  - A snapshot exists from before the write.
  - Other deck slots are byte-identical before and after.
  - Owned costume and character records change only when ticked in the Write
    panel.
- **Explanations.** No explanation contradicts the §6 mechanics. Keep a test
  list of known-wrong claims, such as "Compromise removes Dauntless".
- **CPU ladder.** Opponent quality rises with rank, the top 100 are meta decks,
  and matchmaking still works with the existing point system.
